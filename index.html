AI Automation & Creative Workflow Challenge — Hassan Khawaja

One brief in. A campaign out. Measurement that closes the loop.

Nine areas is not nine tools. It is one pipeline with a shared brief, a creative engine, a production layer, approval gates, and a measurement layer that feeds performance back into the next round of ideas. The demo is the measurement layer, because everything else is only as good as your ability to tell whether it worked.

The loop closes here → performance by angle / hook / format returns to stage 02 as a prior. That is the difference between an automation and a system that gets better.
Six principles that decide the design

One brief schema

Everything downstream reads the same structured brief. No re-typing a client's offer five times into five tools — that is where inconsistency and hallucinated claims come from.

Numbers in code, words in the model

Every metric, threshold and delta is computed deterministically. The LLM explains and prioritises. It never does arithmetic, so it cannot invent a ROAS.

Approve by risk tier, not by habit

Resizing and reporting run alone. Anything that touches paid spend, a client's name, or a regulated claim has a named human in front of it.

Every asset has an ID

Brief → prompt version → model → eval score → live performance. Without that chain you can never tell which idea, hook or model improvement actually worked.

Template the boring 80%

Brand-safe templates, component libraries and locked offer facts remove most failure modes before a model is even involved. Generation is for the 20% that should vary.

Measurement first

It is the cheapest thing to build, the least risky, and it is what makes every other workflow improvable. It is also the fastest way to earn trust in the room.

01

AI Copywriting

Workflow
“Can AI generate high-quality advertising copy consistently, including hooks, headlines, primary text, variations and campaign-ready messaging?”
reads brief · brand kit · hook library  ·  feeds static creative · scripts · landing pages
  1. Brief in one schema — goal, audience, offer, proof points, brand voice, platform, constraints.
  2. Retrieve, don't invent — pull the top-performing hooks and angles for this client from the library, so generation starts from evidence.
  3. 12 variants, structured — 3 angles × (hook, headline, primary text, CTA) at 3 lengths, returned as JSON rather than prose.
  4. Mechanical lint — character limits, banned claims, pricing pulled from the locked offer sheet, readability, duplicate check.
  5. Strategist picks 3 → exported with naming convention and UTMs.
  6. Results by hook and angle return to the library for the next cycle.
Tools
Claude Sonnet (JSON output)Airtable / SheetsLangfuse (prompt versions)LanguageTool
Who does what
AIDrafting, variation volume, length and format adaptation, tone matching, first-pass angle coverage.
HumanFinal selection, claims and pricing accuracy, the client-tone call.
Quality controlSchema validation · no numbers the model can invent (offer facts are locked) · per-vertical banned-claims list · similarity check vs last 90 days · human sign-off.
OutputA campaign-ready copy sheet: 12 drafts → 3 approved, each tagged with angle and hook so results are attributable.
02

AI Static Creative

Workflow
“Can AI generate strong static ad concepts and finished creative assets that are usable for paid advertising, not just generic designs?”
reads idea · brand kit · asset library  ·  feeds Meta · landing page hero
  1. Art direction brief from the approved idea — message hierarchy, format set, brand constraints.
  2. 3 low-fi concepts (Flux / Midjourney) for direction only. This is where a human's taste is cheapest.
  3. Art director picks one. Nothing gets produced in volume before this gate.
  4. Production: generated background and imagery; product, logo and people cut from the licensed asset library; headline, price and legal line composited as real type in an HTML template (Playwright) or the Figma API.
  5. Auto-resize to 1:1, 4:5, 9:16, 16:9 from safe-zone templates.
  6. QA: brand rules, OCR check that rendered text matches the approved copy, safe-zone check, policy check, duplicate check.
  7. Approve → publish via Meta API with the naming taxonomy.
Tools
Flux via fal.aiMidjourney (concepting)Figma API / PlaywrightPhotoroom (cutouts)
Who does what
AIConcepting, backgrounds and imagery, layout variants, resizing and format adaptation.
HumanConcept selection, final art direction, brand sign-off before spend.
Quality controlThe rule that makes this usable: never let the image model draw the logo, the price or the legal line. Real brand assets are composited, so the output is on-brand by construction.
OutputFinished multi-format statics; a small test budget before scaling.
03

AI Script Writing

Workflow
“Can AI produce strong scripts for ads, UGC-style content and short-form video based on campaign goal, audience and offer?”
reads idea · copy · audience insight  ·  feeds UGC video · video editing
  1. Format first — 15s UGC, 30s testimonial, 6s bumper. The format dictates the structure, not the other way round.
  2. 5 hooks, each written to land in under 3 spoken seconds.
  3. Script in beats: hook → problem → mechanism → proof → offer → CTA, with a target duration per beat.
  4. Read-aloud check — words-per-second at 1.0× and 1.15×, so timing is calculated rather than guessed.
  5. Claims, likeness and tone pass before anything is shot or generated.
  6. Storyboard handoff: shot list, on-screen text, b-roll cues.
Tools
Claude OpusElevenLabs (scratch VO)Descript (timing)
Who does what
AIHook volume, beat structure, timing maths, variants per audience.
HumanThe story, claim verification, the final read and tone.
Quality controlDuration is computed from word count and WPS · hook rubric scored against the client's winners · regulated claims reviewed by a human · nothing generated for the presenter without a consent record.
OutputShooting-ready scripts with a shot list and per-beat timing.
04

AI UGC Video

Workflow
“Can AI handle most or all of the UGC production process — from script/idea through to a usable influencer-style video?”
reads script · creator assets  ·  feeds video editing · Meta
  1. Choose the path per concept: real creator (hero content), consented AI avatar (volume), or AI b-roll + text (no face).
  2. Creator path: brief → shot list → footage → edit. Relationships stay human; that is the asset.
  3. AI path: signed avatar + cloned voice → script → lip-sync → b-roll → captions → brand template.
  4. Variation matrix: 3 hooks × 2 CTAs per approved script. This is where AI genuinely beats a shoot on cost.
  5. Human “does it look fake” review and AI-disclosure tagging before spend.
Tools
HeyGen / ArgilElevenLabsRunway / KlingCapCut (polish)
Who does what
AIHook variants, voice, lip-sync, b-roll, captions, localisation.
HumanCreator relationships, authenticity judgement, final approval.
Quality controlWritten consent and likeness release · no fabricated testimonials · platform-required AI disclosure · human review of any health or income claim.
Output6–10 variants per script. Honest view: fully AI UGC still reads as AI. Hybrid — real people for hero, AI for variants and non-face formats — is what performs and what I would build.
05

AI Video Editing

Workflow
“Can AI edit videos from start to finish at a high standard — pacing, cuts, captions, B-roll, audio, branding, export?”
reads raw footage · script  ·  feeds Meta · organic
  1. Ingest + transcribe with word timestamps (Whisper).
  2. Text-based first cut — strip silences and fillers, keep the strongest 3 seconds as the hook.
  3. Beat-matched cuts to the music bed, punch-ins on emphasis.
  4. Branded captions, safe zones, b-roll cutaways from the client library.
  5. Audio: loudness normalisation to −14 LUFS, ducking, SFX.
  6. End card + multi-format export with automated spec checks.
Tools
DescriptFFmpeg (assembly)Auto-EditorDaVinci / CapCutWhisper
Who does what
AITranscription, silence removal, rough cut, captions, B-roll matching, format exports.
HumanPacing and taste, final polish, brand films.
Quality controlAutomated spec checks (duration, codec, bitrate, loudness) · caption accuracy spot check · safe zones · no cut mid-word. Fully automated editing is realistic for templated formats; premium brand films keep a human editor using AI as an assistant.
OutputPlatform-ready 9:16 / 1:1 / 16:9 cuts.
06

AI Media Buyer / Ads Agent

Workflow
“Can an agent monitor campaigns, identify issues, recommend changes, alert, report or take approved actions?”
reads Meta API · account config  ·  feeds Toast · Slack · client reporting
  1. Read hourly Meta Insights and account structure into a warehouse.
  2. Compute metrics and deltas in code — never in the model.
  3. Detect fatigue, CPA inflation, zero-conversion creative, pacing, delivery stalls.
  4. Explain — the LLM writes the “why” from computed facts.
  5. Recommend an action with expected impact and confidence.
  6. Approve in a queue with a before/after snapshot.
  7. Execute via Marketing API with guardrails; log and keep a rollback snapshot.
Tools
Python + Marketing APIn8n schedulerPostgresSlackMetabase
Autonomy tiers
L1 report only → L2 recommend → L3 approved actions → L4 autonomous inside guardrails. CerealBox should be at L3 within 90 days; L4 only after a quarter of clean L3 history.
Quality controlDaily reconciliation against Ads Manager · max N changes per 24h · ≤20% budget delta without approval · allowlist of action types · kill switch · dry-run mode · full audit log.
OutputDaily digest, alert queue, and an action log a client can read.
07

AI Idea Generator

Deep dive
“Can AI consistently generate genuinely interesting campaign ideas that feed copy, static, scripts and video? One of the most important and difficult areas for us.”
reads brief · performance history · creative archive  ·  feeds every production workflow
  1. Give the model real inputs — client context, audience tensions, offer mechanics, last 90 days of creative with performance by angle, seasonality. “Give me 5 ad ideas” is why most output is generic.
  2. Diverge on a fixed taxonomy — insight, contrarian, social proof, stakes, mechanism, identity, urgency, humour — with a cap per angle to force breadth.
  3. Constrain — every idea must work as static, 15s video and 6s bumper, cite a real tension, and not repeat an archived hook.
  4. Novelty filter — embedding similarity against the archive; near-duplicates are killed before a human wastes time on them.
  5. Converge by rubric — insight, audience tension, brand fit, hook strength, feasibility, policy risk. LLM judge first, strategist second.
  6. Promote 3 into production briefs, log the outcome, and use which angles produced winners as next cycle's prior.
Tools
Claude Opuspgvector + embeddingsAirtable idea boardLangfuse traces
Who does what
AIBreadth, combination, first-pass scoring, rationale, archive dedupe.
HumanThe taste call. This is where judgement compounds and should stay.
Quality controlArchive dedupe · mandatory “why it works” rationale · compliance pre-screen · the real metric is idea hit-rate (ideas → winning ads), not volume of ideas.
OutputRanked idea board with production briefs. See the Idea lab tab for the prompt, a scored run, and the kill reasons.
08

AI Landing Pages

Workflow
“Can AI help create high-quality landing pages from a brief, campaign concept or offer — structure, copy, design and iteration?”
reads brief · offer · ad copy  ·  feeds paid traffic · conversion data
  1. Page architecture from the offer: hero, proof, mechanism, offer, objections, FAQ, CTA.
  2. Copy from the same engine as the ads, so the claim on the page matches the claim in the ad — this is the most common conversion leak.
  3. Assemble from an approved component library (brand tokens, accessible, tested) rather than freehand HTML. Speed without broken layouts.
  4. Generate from a template (Astro / Next.js, or Framer when a client needs to self-edit).
  5. Automated QA: Lighthouse, mobile, forms, tracking (pixel + CAPI), legal links.
  6. Staging preview → human approval → publish → A/B test.
Tools
ClaudeNext.js / Astro + TailwindFramerPostHogVercel
Who does what
AIStructure, copy, variants, image selection, first-pass layout.
HumanPublish approval, offer accuracy, brand judgement.
Quality controlNo invented testimonials or statistics · claims match the ads · forms and tracking tested in staging · staging URL reviewed before publish · one variable changed per test.
OutputLive page plus a variant, with conversion data feeding the next brief.

Toast / Meta Ads Analysis

Deep dive · worked demo
“Collect and consolidate ad data, surface key metrics, identify trends and issues, and present useful insights in a clear dashboard.”
reads Meta Marketing API  ·  feeds the media buyer agent · client reporting · creative feedback loop
  1. Ingest hourly Insights pull (or CSV fallback) into Postgres.
  2. Metric layer — deterministic KPIs, week-on-week deltas, pacing against budget.
  3. Rules engine — fatigue, CPA inflation, zero conversion, CTR floor, delivery stall, pacing.
  4. Narrative layer — the LLM writes the explanation from the computed facts only.
  5. Deliver — dashboard, 9am digest, Slack alerts with severity and the money at stake.
  6. Act — recommendation → human approval → Marketing API (dry-run in the demo) → audit log.
  7. Close the loop — outcomes tune thresholds; false positives are fed back explicitly.
Tools
PythonMeta Marketing APIPostgresn8nSlackMetabase
Who does what
AIExplanation, prioritisation, drafting the change, trend narration.
HumanApproves every spend change; sets targets; decides strategy.
Quality controlDaily reconciliation vs Ads Manager (alert if >1% drift) · alert noise budget per client · no action on a single day's data · before/after snapshots · rollback.
OutputThe dashboard on the next tab — 4 accounts, 448 rows, 7 live alerts, an approval queue and an audit log.
Worked demo · area 09

Toast — Meta performance control room

Four client accounts, 28 days of synthetic Meta data, real pipeline. Filter, inspect, approve an action, and watch the audit log.

●
Synthetic data, production architecture. The CSV mirrors the Marketing API insights shape, so the same pipeline runs against a live account with a credential and a column map. Every number below is calculated in Python; the model layer in production only writes the sentences. API calls on approve are simulated — nothing is sent to Meta.
CSV ingest · — rows→ metric layer · deterministic→ rules engine · — alerts→ narrative layer→ dashboard · —

What is deterministic

The KPIs, deltas, thresholds, alert severity, the money at stake and pacing. If a number is wrong, it is a bug in code you can read — not a model's guess.

What the model adds

The “what changed” narrative, prioritising between alerts, and drafting the recommended change in plain language. It receives the computed facts as a JSON payload and is instructed not to recalculate.

What a human keeps

Every spend decision. The queue only proposes. The audit log below is the artifact that makes that defensible to a client.

Deep dive · area 07

Idea lab — the part that decides everything else

Copy, static, scripts and video are production problems. Ideas are a judgement problem. The design goal is to buy breadth cheaply, then spend human taste only on the top of the funnel.

Example brief
Client Calder Foundation · year-end giving
Goal Reactivate lapsed one-time donors (£35–55)
Offer Every gift matched 2× until 31 Dec, cap and terms verified
Constraints No exploitative imagery, no fabricated impact numbers, donor privacy rules
Channels Meta feed + reels, landing page, email
Pipeline
  1. Normalise brief + load 90 days of creative and performance.
  2. Generate 12 ideas across the angle taxonomy, max 2 per angle.
  3. Kill near-duplicates by embedding similarity vs the archive.
  4. Score on the rubric; discard anything that cannot be substantiated.
  5. Human strategist promotes 3 into production briefs.
A scored run — 8 of 12 shown, including what got killed and why

The match has an expiry

mechanismPromoted
“Your £50 becomes £100 at midnight on the 31st. After that, the match is gone.”
Make the deadline the mechanism, not the decoration. Countdown static, 15s UGC, 6s bumper. All three formats read from the same line.
It states the offer's real mechanics and creates urgency without inventing scarcity — the match genuinely ends.
insight brand hook feasible

You stopped giving. The need didn't.

contrarianTest
“In 2023 you gave once. Here's what that one gift is still doing.”
Direct address to lapsed donors, no guilt framing. Testimonials from recipients about a specific programme, not general “impact”.
Speaks to the actual reason donors lapse — they never saw the outcome. Needs a real recipient story; flagged for consent before production.
insight brand hook feasible

The 4-minute ask

insightPromoted
“Most year-end appeals ask for money. This one asks for four minutes.”
A short film about one night at the shelter, then the ask. The ad's job is attention, the page's job is the gift.
Reduces the perceived cost of engaging, and gives a strong reason to click without discounting the offer.
insight brand hook feasible

96 hours

stakesTest
“The gap isn't money. It's 96 hours.”
Countdown-led creative where the unit is time, not pounds. Needs the operational detail to be true and current.
Sharp, but the same urgency territory as idea 1. Serialise them rather than run both at once.
insight brand hook feasible

The donors who came back

social proofPromoted
“1,204 people who stopped giving in 2023 gave again this month. Here's what changed.”
Peer behaviour as the proof. The number must come from the CRM, not from the model.
Lapsed donors assume they are the exception. Social proof normalises returning — strong for this segment.
insight brand hook feasible

Everyone gets a 5× match

stakesKilled
“Every pound you give is matched 5× by an anonymous donor.”
Would probably perform — which is exactly why it is dangerous. The match is 2× and the donor is not anonymous.
Killed by the claims check: contradicts the locked offer facts. This is the failure mode a lint layer exists to catch before a human ever sees it.
insight brand hook risk

Your £50 is worth £100 (again)

mechanismKilled · duplicate
“Double your gift before the year ends.”
0.91 embedding similarity to the June “double your gift” campaign, which had a median ROAS of 0.8.
Killed by the novelty filter before scoring. Repeating a losing hook is the most expensive kind of cheap.
novelty

Lapsed donors who return once are 3× more likely to stay

insightNeeds data
“Coming back once is the whole trick.”
Strong retention framing — but the “3×” is a hypothesis, not a fact from the CRM yet.
Blocked from production until the retention team confirms the number. The model cannot supply it.

The production prompt (abridged)
Show the actual template 
SYSTEM
You are the creative strategist for {{client}}. You generate ad concepts,
not finished copy. Every idea must be executable as a static, a 15s video
and a 6s bumper.

CONTEXT  (injected per run)
- Brand kit and tone rules: {{brand_kit}}
- OFFER FACTS (locked — never contradict, never invent numbers): {{offer}}
- Audience segments and the tension each one feels: {{audiences}}
- Last 90 days of creative: ids, angle, hook, ROAS, CPA  {{archive}}
- Performance priors: angle → median ROAS / CPA  {{priors}}
- Compliance: {{vertical_rules, banned_claims, tone_limits}}

TASK
Return 12 ideas as JSON. For each:
  id, angle (from the fixed taxonomy), audience_segment,
  hook_line (<= 12 words),
  one_line_concept,
  why_it_works (<= 30 words — must name a real tension, not a benefit),
  formats: [static, 15s, 6s],
  production_path: [template | shoot | ai_gen],
  evidence_needed: [] or ["retention rate from CRM"],
  compliance: {risk: low|med|high, reason}

RULES
1. Max 2 ideas per angle. Breadth is the point.
2. Do not reuse or paraphrase any hook in ARCHIVE.
3. No claim that is not in OFFER FACTS. If a number is missing, set
   evidence_needed and do not write the number.
4. Then score every idea 1-10 on insight, audience_tension, brand_fit,
   hook_strength, feasibility, policy_risk (inverted). Return a ranked
   list with a one-line rationale for each score below 6.
Scoring rubric — what a 9 means
InsightSays something the audience has not already heard in this category. 9 = a real tension, stated plainly.
Audience tensionNames a conflict the segment actually feels, not a benefit they want.
Brand fitCould only have come from this client. 3 = could be any charity.
Hook strengthInteresting in 3 seconds, without the image or the logo.
FeasibilityProducible this month with the assets we actually have.
Policy riskInverted. 9 = clean; anything below 5 blocks production.
Honest framing: this run is written out by hand to show the shape and judgement. The prompt is the artifact I would ship; the scoring is done by an LLM judge, then overruled by a strategist — the overrules are the training signal for the rubric.
Execution

90-day plan, cost, and what breaks

Sequenced so each milestone makes the next one cheaper. Measurement first, because it is low-risk and it is what tells you whether the creative work is any good.

Why this order

Measurement before generation

Without Toast you cannot tell whether AI creative is working. It is also the fastest, least political win and it builds the data that later feeds the idea engine.

Copy before visuals, ideas before volume

Copy is the cheapest end-to-end proof of the brief schema. Ideas come before production volume so you are not industrialising mediocre concepts — and the agent comes last, because it needs trustworthy data plus an approval habit.

WEEKS 1–2

Foundations

  • n8n (self-hosted) + Postgres + one shared brief schema; brand kits for the three largest clients.
  • Toast v1: hourly Meta pull → rules → 9am digest + dashboard for two pilot clients.
  • Copy v1: brand voice, claims lint, variant sheet.
Done whenThe digest lands at 09:00 unattended, reconciles within 1% of Ads Manager and is used daily by one buyer. Three paid briefs have run through the copy pipeline.
DAY 30

Creative core

  • Idea generator v1: taxonomy, archive dedupe, rubric scoring; a weekly 45-minute idea session.
  • Static v1: three master templates, brand-safe text compositing, 10 concepts/week.
  • Landing pages: component library + two live pages.
Done whenBrief → ideas → copy → static runs end to end for one client in under 48h, and at least half of the concepts survive review.
DAY 60

Volume

  • Scripts + UGC hybrid pilot: real hero creators, AI hook and CTA variants, consent and disclosure workflow.
  • Video editing: templated FFmpeg assembly, auto captions, loudness and spec QC, under 24h turnaround.
  • Toast v2: recommendations → Slack approval → Marketing API in dry-run.
Done when20 variants/month from one brief, zero unapproved spend changes, edit QC pass rate above 90%.
DAY 90

Closed loop

  • Media buyer at L3 on two accounts: approved actions, before/after snapshots, rollback, kill switch.
  • Performance → priors: angle, hook and format results feed the idea engine; monthly prompt and model eval.
  • Client portal and per-client cost attribution.
Done whenAt least 25% of recommendations are approved unchanged, CPA improves against a control cohort, and the false-positive alert rate is under 5%.

What breaks, and the mitigation
Samey outputArchive dedupe, per-client style memory, human art direction at concept stage.
Hallucinated numbersMetrics and offer facts computed or locked in code; the model explains only.
Model drift / vendor changePrompts and eval sets in Git, LiteLLM gateway, pinned versions, monthly regression run.
Meta API or policy changeAdapter layer with a CSV fallback, sandbox app, no live access without approval.
Likeness, disclosure, music rightsConsent log, AI-disclosure tags, licensed library, legal checklist per vertical.
Over-automation erodes trustApproval tiers, visible audit log, a human name on anything client-facing.
Attribution errorNever act on one day; 7-day windows, reconciliation, and a stated confidence.

If I joined — the first 90 days, personally

I would own Toast and the brief schema myself, because everything else depends on them, and pair with one buyer and one strategist rather than rolling out to everyone at once.

Days 1–14 — two pilot clients live on Toast; brief schema agreed; copy pipeline running.
Days 15–45 — idea engine and static templates in a weekly rhythm; measure idea hit-rate from week one.
Days 46–90 — UGC hybrid pilot, video assembly, and the media buyer at L3 with guardrails.

What I would build next

1. Consolidate into one internal “CerealBox Studio” — one brief in, all assets out, one approval queue.
2. Creative performance loop: automatically read results into angle × format × hook priors.
3. Compliance packs per vertical, so regulated clients get a tuned reviewer rather than a generic one.
4. Client-facing portal — the reports you already have, in a login, cutting account-manager hours.
5. One named owner for the system. It needs an owner more than it needs another tool.
What I would want to know from you: which two clients are most painful to report on today, what the media buyers actually do in the first hour of their day, and where a wrong AI-generated claim would be least forgivable. Those three answers would change the order of the roadmap more than any tool choice.
